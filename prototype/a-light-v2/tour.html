About

Half a day under the volcano.

The El Ceibo forest loop, the 1968 lava flow and the lake viewpoint, with a certified naturalist guide and a spotting scope. The cone is usually clear until around ten and clouded by early afternoon — that single fact sets the whole timetable, which is why we leave at eight and are back before lunch.

The day

Out by eight, back before lunch.

  1. 08:00

    Pick-up in La Fortuna

    From your hotel lobby. Fifteen minutes to the park gate on Route 142, then a signposted gravel access road.

  2. 08:30

    El Ceibo trail · 1.8 km

    Flat, shaded, secondary forest to a ceibo several hundred years old. The best of the three walks for sloths and birds.

  3. 10:00

    The 1968 lava flow · 2.6 km

    Out onto the flow itself. Loose black rock, no shade, and the closest you get to the cone.

  4. 11:15

    Lake viewpoint

    Last, deliberately — by now the volcano has usually clouded over.

  5. 12:00

    Back in town

    Dropped where you were collected.

Scope

Included, excluded, what to bring.

Included

  • Round-trip transport from your hotel in La Fortuna
  • Entrance to Arenal Volcano National Park
  • Certified naturalist guide, English and Spanish
  • Spotting scope and bottled water

Not included

  • Meals — the walk ends before lunch
  • Gratuities
  • Personal travel insurance

Bring with you

  • Closed shoes with grip — the lava flow is loose rock
  • Sun protection; there is no shade on the flow
  • A rain layer, year round
  • Insect repellent for the forest loop

Honestly

What we say before you book.

  • You will not see lava. Arenal stopped erupting in 2010 — anyone selling a “lava tour” is selling a walk on rock that cooled decades ago.
  • The cone is often invisible. It makes its own weather. If it is clear when you wake up, move your plans and come that morning.
  • The park is the quiet option. Most visitors photograph the volcano from the road and never pay to walk under it.
  • The trails are self-guided and signposted. What you are buying is the naturalist and the scope, not access.
